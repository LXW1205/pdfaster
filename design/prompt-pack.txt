# pdfaster · Hi-Fi Instrument — prompt pack for follow-up restyles

Voice for future graphic-only passes on this repo (design/DESIGN.md is the contract;
design/tokens.css is the source of truth; `@theme inline` in src/styles/theme.css wires
it). All prompts assume: graphics and animations only — no features, no copy, no logic,
no router/state/lib changes.

1. "Make the tool grid feel like a rack unit: gap the ToolPicker into labelled bays with
   engraved grooves between channels and screw dots on the rail ends; keep one signal
   orange on the engaged tool only."
2. "Add a VU-meter-style progress state to the compress tool: a static-segment mono
   readout bar that fills during the run, engine needle pinned at the end for a 600ms
   hold, then settles. Signal orange fill, ink labels, no looping animation."
3. "Give the editor thumbnails a channel strip treatment: surface-2 casework, hairline
   groove dividers, mono page numbers, and one top-lit radial only on the active thumb."
4. "Turn the hero into the machine's faceplate: screw dots at the corners, an engraved
   hairline circle behind the headline, and a red LED dot beside 'stays on your device'
   that lights signal orange while a tool is running."
5. "Introduce the dial-toplight treatment on circular controls only: undo/redo become
   engraved hairline circles with a radial top-light at 35% 28%; pressed state sinks
   2px and deepens the inset. Do not invent new circular controls."
6. "Rebuild the FileDropZone as an input cassette bay: dashed groove is the cassette
   slot, idle = surface-2 inset, drag-engaged = signal hairline; numeric readout under
   the slot in mono for file count and size."
7. "Engrave the settings rows on Watermark/Page numbers: 1px grooves between control
   fields, caps mono micro-labels left, numeric readouts right in tabular mono."
8. "Audit the app for a second orange: any state where two signal-orange elements are
   visible in one group must demote the older one to ink-on-cream. Report the pairs you
   find and the token you used instead."
